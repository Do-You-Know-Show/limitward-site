Risk-reward and break-even win rate

Your win rate on its own tells you very little. What matters is how a win compares with a loss. That relationship is the risk-reward ratio.

The formulas

  • Risk-reward = (target − entry) ÷ (entry − stop), for a long trade. A $2 stop and a $5 target is 2.5.
  • Break-even win rate = 1 ÷ (1 + risk-reward), before costs.
  • Expectancy in R = win rate × average win in R − loss rate × average loss in R.

A strategy that risks 1 to make 2 only needs to win about one trade in three to break even, before costs. A strategy that risks 1 to make 0.5 needs to win two in three.

Worked example

Suppose a setup wins 40% of the time, wins an average of 2.5R and loses an average of 1R. Expectancy = 0.4 × 2.5 − 0.6 × 1 = 0.4R per trade. That is a positive number on paper. It says nothing about whether your real results will match your assumptions, and a small sample can mislead you either way.

Costs move the line

Fees and slippage reduce each win and increase each loss, so the real break-even win rate is higher than the table. This matters most on tight stops, where costs are a large share of the risk.

Frequently asked questions

What win rate do I need to break even?

Break-even win rate equals 1 divided by (1 + risk-reward ratio), before costs. At 2-to-1 it is about 33%, at 1-to-1 it is 50%.

Is a high win rate better than a high risk-reward?

Neither is better on its own. Expectancy combines both, and costs move the line, so look at the two together.

What is expectancy?

The average result per trade in units of risk (R): win rate times average win, minus loss rate times average loss.
