How to set a daily loss limit

A daily loss limit is a number you decide in advance that ends your trading day. The number matters less than whether you actually stop when you reach it.

Two different limits

A prop firm's daily loss limit is a rule that can end your account. Your personal daily limit is a rule you set to stay well away from it. Your personal limit should sit inside the firm's, with room to spare, because fees, slippage and open positions can carry a loss past the line you drew.

Ways to choose a number

  • A fraction of your account. For example a small fixed percentage of the account.
  • A multiple of your per-trade risk. For example three times the amount you risk on one trade, which also caps the number of full losers in a row.
  • A fraction of the firm's buffer. The drawdown calculator uses half of your remaining room, split across a run of two full stop-outs. On a $50,000 account with a $5,000 static buffer that works out to $1,250 per trade.

None of these is a recommendation. They are ways to turn a vague intention into a number you can check.

Write the rule before the session

Research on implementation intentions, plans of the form "if X happens, then I do Y", found that they improve goal attainment, with a medium-to-large average effect (Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006). A trading version: "If I am down my daily limit, then I close the platform for the day." Writing it when you are calm is the point.

Enforcement beats willpower

A rule you can switch off in the moment tends to get switched off in the moment. Some brokers and platforms offer server-side daily loss limits that automatically stop trading, for example Tradovate's daily loss limit. If yours does, use it. An app or a note on your monitor is a reminder, not a lock, and anyone who tells you otherwise is overselling.

What to do when you hit it

  • Stop placing trades. Close or flatten positions according to your plan.
  • Write down what happened in two sentences while it is fresh.
  • Do not review the day for ideas on how to win it back. That is the subject of the revenge trading guide.

Frequently asked questions

How big should a daily loss limit be?

There is no universal number. Common approaches are a small percentage of the account, a multiple of your per-trade risk, or a fraction of the room left before your prop firm's limit. Pick one you can write down and check.

Is a daily loss limit the same as a prop firm's daily loss rule?

No. The firm's rule is a hard limit that can end your account. Your personal limit should be tighter, so a bad day ends well before the firm's line.

Can an app stop me from trading after I hit my limit?

An app that only tracks your numbers cannot block orders at your broker. Broker-side limits can. Treat a tracker as a reminder and use a broker-level limit if one is available.
