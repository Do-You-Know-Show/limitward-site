How to stop revenge trading

Revenge trading is taking a trade to win back a loss rather than because it meets your plan. It usually follows a loss, and it usually comes with a bigger size.

The pattern

One loss, then a trade that is slightly outside your criteria, justified as close enough. Then a larger size to recover faster, then another loss. Nobody decides to blow up an account in one trade. The damage builds across several small rule breaks, each of which felt reasonable at the time.

Why more trading tends to cost more

In a well-known study of US brokerage accounts, Barber and Odean (Journal of Finance, 2000) found that the households that traded most earned about 11.4% a year net of costs, against 17.9% for the market. Trading more is not the same as trading better, and costs compound against you. That study covers investors in general, not prop-firm traders, but the lesson about overtrading carries.

A practical rule set

  1. Write the rules before the session. Maximum risk per trade, daily loss limit, maximum number of trades, and what you do when you hit them.
  2. Stop after N losses in a row. Two or three is common. The point is to stop before your judgment is at its worst.
  3. Take a cooldown after a loss. Fifteen to thirty minutes away from the screen is a common guideline. The evidence on the best length is thin, so treat it as a starting point and use whatever interrupts the urge for you.
  4. Never increase size after a loss. If anything, size down.
  5. Use a broker-side limit if you have one. A limit you cannot undo in the moment is worth more than a promise to yourself.
  6. Do a two-line review. What did I do, and did it match the plan? Skip the part where you plan how to recover.

Why a written if-then plan helps

Plans of the form "if this happens, then I do that" have a medium-to-large effect on following through on goals (Gollwitzer and Sheeran, 2006). "If I lose two in a row, then I stand up and leave the desk for thirty minutes" is the kind of plan that works because the decision was made in advance.

Know your numbers first

Revenge trades are often oversized because the trader has no idea how much room they actually have. Work it out with the drawdown calculator and set your daily loss limit before you start.

Frequently asked questions

What is revenge trading?

Taking a trade to win back a loss instead of because it meets your plan, often with a larger size than usual.

How long should a cooldown after a loss be?

Fifteen to thirty minutes is a common guideline, but there is little firm evidence for an ideal length. Use whatever reliably breaks the urge, and decide the length in advance.

Does a trading journal stop revenge trading?

A journal helps you notice the pattern afterwards. Stopping it in the moment needs a rule or a limit you set beforehand, such as a stop-after-losses rule or a broker-side limit.
