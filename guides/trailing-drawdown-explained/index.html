Trailing drawdown explained

A drawdown limit is a floor under your account. If your equity touches it, the challenge or funded account is over. The confusing part is that the floor can behave in three different ways.

The three common types

  • Static: the floor is fixed at the starting balance minus the maximum loss. It never moves, even if you make money. FTMO's 2-Step Challenge uses a static maximum loss of 10% of initial capital.
  • End-of-day (EOD) trailing: the floor follows your highest end-of-day balance upward and never moves down. Topstep's Maximum Loss Limit in the Trading Combine works this way.
  • Intraday trailing: the floor follows your highest equity during the day, including open profit. Some firms and products use this. It is the least forgiving type, because a profit you did not close can still raise your floor.

Worked example: Topstep $50K Combine

Per Topstep, the Maximum Loss Limit starts $2,000 below the account size on a $50K Combine, trails your end-of-day balance upward, never moves down, and locks once it reaches your starting balance. Both realized and unrealized profit and loss count toward the limit in real time. The floor only updates at the end of the day.

Worked example: FTMO $100K 2-Step

FTMO's Max Loss is a static 10% of initial capital, so on a $100,000 account the floor is $90,000 and it does not move. Its Max Daily Loss is 5% of initial capital, measured on equity, recalculated at 00:00 CE(S)T from your balance at that time.

Suppose your balance at the start of the day is $102,000. The daily floor is $97,000. If your equity is $100,500, you have $3,500 of room before the daily limit, and that is the limit that matters today because it is closer than the $90,000 max-loss floor. Run it in the FTMO drawdown calculator.

Which limit is binding?

When a firm has both a daily limit and a maximum limit, the closer floor is the one that can end your day. Always measure your room against the nearer one.

What this means for your position size

Your real risk budget is not the account size. It is the room between your equity and the nearest floor. A planning habit that helps: use only part of that room, and make sure a short run of full stop-outs still leaves you inside it. The prop-firm drawdown calculator does this, and the position size guide turns the resulting dollar risk into contracts, lots or shares.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing balance with equity. Open profit and loss count toward most limits.
  • Assuming every product from one firm has the same rules. Different products use different percentages and trailing styles.
  • Forgetting fees and slippage, which make a stop-out cost more than the planned risk.
  • Relying on a summary like this one instead of the firm's current rules.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between static and trailing drawdown?

A static drawdown floor is fixed at the starting balance minus the maximum loss and never moves. A trailing drawdown floor rises as your balance or equity reaches new highs, and it does not come back down.

Do open profits move a trailing drawdown?

It depends on the firm. Topstep updates its Combine floor from the end-of-day balance but counts open profit and loss toward the limit in real time. Intraday trailing limits follow equity during the day. Check the rules for the exact product you bought.

Where can I check the exact rules?

On the firm's own help pages. Our firm pages link to the source we checked and show the date, for example the Topstep and FTMO pages.
