How to calculate position size

Position sizing starts from one decision: how much you are willing to lose if the trade hits your stop. Everything else is arithmetic.

The one idea

Size = dollars you are willing to risk ÷ dollars lost per unit if the stop is hit. Round down, never up, so the result cannot exceed your limit.

Stocks

Risk per share is the distance between entry and stop, plus any per-share costs. On a $25,000 account risking 1% ($250), with entry 50 and stop 48, the risk per share is $2, so the size is 125 shares. If you also pay a $2 round-trip commission, the budget for price risk is $248 and the size drops to 124 shares. Small costs matter at small sizes.

Futures

Tick value = contract multiplier × tick size. Risk per contract = stop distance in ticks × tick value + fees. For ES, a 4-point stop is 16 ticks of $12.50, or $200 per contract, so a $500 budget buys 2 contracts. With a $4.50 round-trip fee and a $400 budget, the cost per contract is $204.50 and the size is 1 contract. Without the fee the same trade would look like 2 contracts. See the futures tick values guide.

Forex

Pip value per unit = pip size × the rate that converts the quote currency into your account currency. On EURUSD with a US dollar account a standard lot is worth $10 per pip, so a 20-pip stop with a $200 budget gives 1.00 standard lot.

Crypto

Quantity = risk ÷ (stop distance + fees per unit). Entry 60,000, stop 58,800 and a $300 budget gives 0.25 BTC, a position worth $15,000. On a leveraged perpetual, margin is the position value divided by leverage, and your stop should sit well inside the liquidation price.

Why fees and rounding matter

Costs are part of the stop-out, so include them. And because you cannot trade a fraction of a contract, always round down. Rounding to the nearest whole number can quietly push your real risk above your limit.

Frequently asked questions

What is the position size formula?

Position size equals the dollars you are willing to risk divided by the dollars lost per unit if the stop is hit, rounded down.

How much of my account should I risk per trade?

There is no single correct number. Many traders choose a small fixed percentage of the account, and prop-firm traders also need to stay inside the firm's drawdown limits. Limitward does not recommend a figure; it calculates the size for whatever you choose.

Should I include fees in position sizing?

Yes. Commissions and slippage are part of what a stop-out costs, and ignoring them can put your real loss above your planned risk.
